## Scandinavian art books: where to start

Start broad. [*Art of the Nordic Nations*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/0500029563) by Serge Fauchereau (Thames & Hudson, 2025) is about the closest thing to a single-volume survey of the region's art in the years it found its voice, 1870 to 1950, and at 464 pages it has room to do it properly. It's the one to reach for when you want to understand why Nordic art looks the way it does: the long northern light, the quiet interiors, the pull between clarity and melancholy that runs through so much of it.

From there, follow the light to the painters themselves. Vilhelm Hammershøi built a whole career out of empty rooms and grey Copenhagen daylight, and once you've sat with his interiors you start noticing that calm everywhere. Harald Sohlberg and Harriet Backer gave the Norwegian landscape and the lamplit room the same still attention. Munch you already know, but he repays a proper monograph rather than a fridge magnet of The Scream. When one of these painters asks for a closer look, we've picked out [six Nordic artist monographs worth living with](/article/nordic-artist-monographs).

## Are Nordic and Scandinavian the same thing?

No, and the difference is why this series says Nordic. [Scandinavia is the smaller word](https://nordics.info/show/artikel/the-nordic-region): Denmark, Norway and Sweden, and some people stretch it no further than the Norwegian and Swedish peninsula. The Nordic region takes in those three plus Finland and Iceland, and the Faroe Islands, Greenland and Åland with them. Fauchereau works to the wider definition, all five nations in one volume, and so does everything below. Narrow the shelf to Scandinavian and you'd lose Finnish glass and most of the photography, which would be a strange thing to do on purpose. If you want the longer answer about the art rather than the map, here's [what Scandinavian art actually is](/article/what-is-scandinavian-art).

## Which Scandinavian design books are worth owning?

Then there's design, which in the Nordic countries was never really treated as separate from art. [*Scandinavian Home*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/1849497494) by Elizabeth Wilhide is a clear, country-by-country guide to the mid-century furniture and interiors the region is famous for. [*New Nordic Houses*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/0500021554) by Dominic Bradbury carries that instinct into the present, all cabins in the woods and coastal retreats, shot beautifully enough to frame.

## Nordic interior design books for the coffee table

If you want the feeling more than the history, [*North: How to Live Scandinavian*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/178131652X) by Brontë Aurell and [*The Monocle Book of the Nordics*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/0500971218) sit at the warmer, browsable end of the shelf: design, food and the small habits that make the region what it is. These are the two to leave out where people will pick them up.
